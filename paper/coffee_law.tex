\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent systems built from large language models (LLMs) are often expected
to produce knowledge beyond any single agent through debate, critique and
voting. We give information-theoretic ceilings on what such collectives can
achieve, independent of how the agents work. First, the \emph{Coffee Law}: for
any number of agents, any communication protocol and any adaptive tool-query
policy, the information the collective holds about an external truth $\Theta$
is at most $\min\{\Hh(\Theta),\, \I(\Theta;D_0)+\sum_t C_t\}$, where $D_0$ is
the starting data and $C_t$ is the Shannon capacity of round $t$'s tool calls.
Fano's inequality turns this into a floor on error and a lower bound on the
number of tool rounds. Second, a \emph{reasoning ceiling}: the information that
computationally bounded agents can \emph{use} is at most what they \emph{hold},
with the gap equal to an expected KL divergence; computation, including debate,
can raise usable information but never available information. Third,
\emph{computational bounds} on closing that gap: only a collective's total
compute matters, parity requires reading every input, and under standard
cryptographic assumptions some fully available information is unusable by any
polynomial-time collective. We then extend the law to compute-gated
channels (the formal content of sandboxing), worst-case tasks, acting,
communication between agents, a drifting truth, adversaries and continuous
quantities. The ceilings account for published findings that extra test-time compute does not
help on knowledge-intensive tasks, that debate helps on reasoning tasks, and
that debate performs about as well as voting at equal compute. Every finite
example is verified exactly in public code.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

LLM-based agent systems combine many model instances that post to a shared
board, debate, vote and call tools such as search engines, code interpreters and
APIs~\cite{du2024,yao2023,schick2023}. A common expectation is that such a
collective can know more than any member. This paper asks how much more, and
from where.

We answer with two ceilings that hold for \emph{any} agents---LLMs, people or
programs---because the proofs never inspect the agents:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The Coffee Law} (Theorem~\ref{thm:coffee}). A collective can never
know more about the world than its starting data plus what its tools can carry.
Communication and internal computation add nothing to this budget; only starting
data and informative tool calls raise it. The number of agents does not appear.
\item \textbf{The reasoning ceiling} (Theorem~\ref{thm:reasoning}). What
computationally bounded agents can \emph{use} is at most what they \emph{hold}.
Reasoning, including debate, closes this gap but never passes it.
\end{itemize}
Together they give the chain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain}
\underbrace{\I_{\mathcal V}(D_T\!\to\!\Theta)}_{\text{usable}}
\le \underbrace{\I(\Theta;D_T)}_{\text{available}}
\le \underbrace{\min\{\Hh(\Theta),\,B_T\}}_{\text{budget}},
\end{equation}
with $B_T=\I(\Theta;D_0)+\sum_{t<T}C_t$, where reasoning raises the left term
and tools raise the right one. Our
contributions are: (i)~the capacity budget for tool-using collectives with
adaptive queries, with a Fano error floor and a tightness example; (ii)~the
combination of available and usable information into one chain, with an exact
expression for the reasoning gap; (iii)~computational bounds on closing that
gap, from above by total compute and from below unconditionally (parity) and
conditionally (a hard-core bit); (iv)~extensions that relax each modeling
assumption (Section~\ref{sec:ext}); (v)~an account of several published
empirical findings on debate and test-time compute
(Section~\ref{sec:empirical}); and (vi)~exact numerical verification of every
finite example.\footnote{Code, proofs and checks:
\url{https://github.com/mbhatt1/coffeelaw}.}

The ingredients---the chain rule, the data-processing inequality (DPI), channel
capacity and Fano's inequality---are classical~\cite{cover2006,shannon1948}. The
contribution is their assembly into a model of tool-using agent collectives and
the resulting separation of information-bound from compute-bound tasks.

\section{Model}\label{sec:model}

All variables are discrete with finite support; logarithms are base~2.
$\Theta$ is a fixed external truth. $D_t$ is the collective's \emph{full record}
at the start of round $t$: the initial data $D_0$ (every agent's context and
weights) plus every post, private state, query and tool result since. Each
agent's state and the board are functions of $D_t$. In round $t$, fresh
internal randomness $\xi_t$ (sampling, scheduling) is drawn; the round's tool
calls $Q_t=g_t(D_t,\xi_t)$ are issued under any policy $g_t$; results
$O_{t+1}\sim K_t(\cdot\mid\Theta,Q_t,D_t)$ return; communication and computation
use fresh randomness $\zeta_t$; and
\[
D_{t+1}=(D_t,\xi_t,Q_t,O_{t+1},\zeta_t).
\]

\noindent\textbf{Assumptions.}
(A1)~\emph{No side channels}: $\Theta$ reaches the collective only through $D_0$
and tool results.
(A2)~\emph{Truth-independent noise}: $\xi_t\perp(\Theta,D_t)$ and
$\zeta_t\perp(\Theta,D_t,\xi_t,Q_t,O_{t+1})$.
(A3)~\emph{Tools are channels from $\Theta$}: given $(\Theta,Q_t,D_t)$, $O_{t+1}$
is independent of $\xi_t$ with law $K_t$; tools may be adaptive or stateful.
(A4)~\emph{Fixed truth}: actions do not change $\Theta$.

\noindent\textbf{Capacity.} For a query $q$ and history $d$, $\theta\mapsto
K_t(\cdot\mid\theta,q,d)$ is a channel with capacity
$\mathrm{Cap}(K_{t,q,d})=\max_{P_\Theta}\I(\Theta;O)$. Let
$C_t := \sup_{q,d}\mathrm{Cap}(K_{t,q,d}) \le \log|\mathcal O_t|$, where
$\mathcal O_t$ is the set of possible results in round $t$.

\section{Conservation and Synergy}

\begin{proposition}[Conservation]\label{prop:conservation}
If no tools are called ($C_t=0$ for all $t$), then $\I(\Theta;D_T)\le \I(\Theta;D_0)$.
\end{proposition}
This is the special case of Theorem~\ref{thm:coffee} with no tools; it also
follows directly from the DPI, since $D_T$ is a function of $D_0$ and
$\Theta$-independent noise. Debate, critique and voting therefore add no
information about $\Theta$.

\begin{proposition}[Synergy]\label{prop:synergy}
Let $B_1,B_2$ be i.i.d.\ uniform bits and $\Theta=B_1\oplus B_2$. Then
$\I(\Theta;B_1)=\I(\Theta;B_2)=0$ but $\I(\Theta;B_1,B_2)=1$.
\end{proposition}
Pooling can make jointly held information accessible that no member can use
alone; this is the failure of submodularity of $S\mapsto \I(\Theta;S)$ studied
in partial information decomposition~\cite{williams2010}. The bit was present
in $D_0$ throughout: emergence rearranges the budget without enlarging it.

\section{The Coffee Law}\label{sec:coffee}

\begin{theorem}[Coffee Law]\label{thm:coffee}
Under (A1)--(A4), let $B_T := \I(\Theta;D_0)+\sum_{t<T}C_t$. Then
\[
\I(\Theta;D_T)\le \min\{\Hh(\Theta),\,B_T\},
\]
and the same bound holds for every output $Y$ of the collective (answer, board,
vote, any agent's state). For any estimator $\hat\Theta$ computed from the
record, with $|\Theta|\ge 3$,
\[
\Pr(\hat\Theta\ne\Theta)\ \ge\ \frac{\Hh(\Theta)-\min\{\Hh(\Theta),B_T\}-1}{\log(|\Theta|-1)} .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the chain rule, $\I(\Theta;D_{t+1})-\I(\Theta;D_t)$ equals
\begin{multline*}
\I(\Theta;\xi_t|D_t)+\I(\Theta;Q_t|D_t,\xi_t)\\
+\I(\Theta;O_{t+1}|S)+\I(\Theta;\zeta_t|S,O_{t+1}),
\end{multline*}
with $S=(D_t,\xi_t,Q_t)$. The first and last terms vanish by (A2); the second
vanishes because $Q_t$ is a function of $(D_t,\xi_t)$. For the third, fix
$S=s=(d,x,q)$. By (A3), given $S=s$ the pair $(\Theta,O_{t+1})$ has $\Theta\sim
p(\cdot|s)$ and $O_{t+1}\sim K_t(\cdot|\Theta,q,d)$, so
$\I(\Theta;O_{t+1}|S=s)$ is the mutual information of channel $K_{t,q,d}$ under
input law $p(\cdot|s)$, at most its capacity and hence at most $C_t$. Averaging
over $s$ and summing over rounds gives $\I(\Theta;D_T)\le B_T$; also
$\I(\Theta;D_T)\le\Hh(\Theta)$. Outputs are functions of $D_T$ and independent
noise, so the DPI extends the bound to them. For the error floor, Fano's
inequality~\cite{cover2006} gives $\Hh(\Theta|\hat\Theta)\le 1+\Pr(\hat\Theta\ne\Theta)\log(|\Theta|-1)$,
and the DPI gives $\Hh(\Theta|\hat\Theta)\ge \Hh(\Theta)-\min\{\Hh(\Theta),B_T\}$.
\end{proof}

Adaptivity is already accounted for: the query policy only selects the input
law $p(\cdot|s)$ and the channel $K_{t,q,d}$, and capacity is the maximum over
both. This mirrors the classical fact that feedback does not increase the
capacity of a memoryless channel~\cite{shannon1956,massey1990} and the use of Fano's
inequality for active-learning lower bounds~\cite{raginsky2011}.

\begin{corollary}[Rounds needed]\label{cor:rounds}
If $\Theta$ is uniform on $2^k$ values ($k\ge 2$), $\I(\Theta;D_0)=0$ and $C_t\le
C$ for all $t$, then reaching $\Pr(\hat\Theta\ne\Theta)\le\varepsilon$ requires
$T\ge ((1-\varepsilon)k-1)/C$ tool rounds, regardless of the number of agents
or how they communicate.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Agents do not appear]
Synergy, debate, voting and the number of agents $N$ are absent from $B_T$. More
agents raise the budget only by issuing more or better tool calls, i.e.\ by
raising $C_t$.
\end{corollary}

\noindent\textbf{Tightness.} Let $\Theta$ be uniform on $\{0,1,2,3\}$ and let a
call $q\in\{0,1\}$ return bit $q$ of $\Theta$ through a binary symmetric channel
with flip probability $0.1$, so $C=1-h(0.1)\approx 0.531$ bits. Table~\ref{tab:tight}
gives exact values. Querying both bits attains the budget; an adaptive policy
that sometimes re-queries the same bit falls below it; a debate step adds
nothing.

\noindent\textbf{Exact accounting and when the law is tight.} The proof gives
the identity $\I(\Theta;D_T)=\I(\Theta;D_0)+\sum_t\E_s[\I(\Theta;O_{t+1}|S_t=s)]$,
and each term is at most the capacity of the channel actually used, which is at
most $\min\{\log|\mathcal O_t|,\log|\mathrm{supp}\,\Theta|\}$. Equality in
Theorem~\ref{thm:coffee} holds iff almost every posterior is a
capacity-achieving input for the chosen channel and that channel attains $C_t$.
For rich tools the binding quantity is the realized gain; the capacity form bites
for low-capacity tools. \emph{Example:} a test run returns pass or fail, so it
carries at most 1 bit. Locating one bad commit among $n$ with error at most
$\varepsilon$ needs at least $(1-\varepsilon)\log n-1$ runs
(Corollary~\ref{cor:rounds}), whatever agents run the search; binary search
(\texttt{git bisect}) uses $\lceil\log n\rceil$. A flaky test that misreports
with probability $p$ raises the minimum by the factor $1/(1-h(p))$.

\section{The Reasoning Ceiling}\label{sec:reasoning}

Holding information is not the same as being able to use it: the prime factors
of $N$ are fully determined by $N$, yet finding them may take enormous
computation. Following Xu~et~al.~\cite{xu2020}, we model bounded agents by a
predictive family.

\begin{definition}
A \emph{predictive family} $\mathcal V$ is a set of predictors $f$ mapping a
record $d$ to a distribution $f[d]$ on $\Theta$, containing the constant prior
predictor $f[d]=p_\Theta$. Define
$\Hh_{\mathcal V}(\Theta|D) := \inf_{f\in\mathcal V}\E[-\log f[D](\Theta)]$ and
the \emph{usable information}
$\I_{\mathcal V}(D\to\Theta) := \Hh(\Theta)-\Hh_{\mathcal V}(\Theta|D)$.
\end{definition}

Xu~et~al.\ use the family's own no-input entropy as the baseline; with the prior
predictor in $\mathcal V$, as assumed here, the baseline is $\Hh(\Theta)$.

\begin{theorem}[Reasoning ceiling]\label{thm:reasoning}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $0\le \I_{\mathcal V}(D\to\Theta)\le \I(\Theta;D)$, and the
\emph{reasoning gap} is
\begin{multline*}
\I(\Theta;D)-\I_{\mathcal V}(D\to\Theta)\\
=\inf_{f\in\mathcal V}\E_D\big[\KL\big(p(\cdot|D)\,\|\,f[D]\big)\big].
\end{multline*}
\item[(b)] Under (A1)--(A4), the chain~\eqref{eq:chain} holds for the record $D_T$.
\item[(c)] There exist $\mathcal V$ and a deterministic $g$ with
$\I(\Theta;g(D))=\I(\Theta;D)$ but $\I_{\mathcal V}(g(D)\to\Theta)>\I_{\mathcal V}(D\to\Theta)$.
For every $g$, $\I_{\mathcal V}(g(D)\to\Theta)\le \I(\Theta;D)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a)~The prior predictor gives $\Hh_{\mathcal V}(\Theta|D)\le\Hh(\Theta)$. For any
$f$, the cross-entropy identity gives
$\E[-\log f[D](\Theta)]=\Hh(\Theta|D)+\E_D\KL(p(\cdot|D)\|f[D])$; take the
infimum and subtract from $\Hh(\Theta)$. (b)~Part~(a) and
Theorem~\ref{thm:coffee}. (c)~The last claim is (a) followed by the DPI. For the
strict example take Proposition~\ref{prop:synergy} with $D=(B_1,B_2)$ and the
``one-look'' family $\mathcal V_1$ of predictors that read a single coordinate of
the record. Each coordinate of $D$ is independent of $\Theta$, so
$\I_{\mathcal V_1}(D\to\Theta)=0$. After one reasoning step
$g(D)=(B_1\oplus B_2,B_2)$ the first coordinate equals $\Theta$, so
$\I_{\mathcal V_1}(g(D)\to\Theta)=1=\I(\Theta;g(D))=\I(\Theta;D)$.
\end{proof}

Part~(c) is the observation of~\cite{xu2020} that usable information violates
the DPI, placed in the collective setting.

\begin{corollary}[Debate adds reasoning, not facts]
In a closed collective every post is a function of $D_0$ and noise, so it adds
no available information (Proposition~\ref{prop:conservation}). It can add usable
information: an agent that posts an intermediate result hands others a
computation outside their family. The gain is capped by $\I(\Theta;D_0)$.
\end{corollary}

\noindent\textbf{Two regimes.} A task is \emph{information-bound} when the right
inequality of~\eqref{eq:chain} binds (forecasting, facts about the world,
diagnosing a live system): only tools and better starting data help. It is
\emph{compute-bound} when the left gap is large (mathematics, proofs, code,
planning from known facts): more reasoning helps. A code interpreter applied to
data already held enlarges $\mathcal V$; a tool that observes the world raises
$C_t$. In the noisy example of Table~\ref{tab:tight}, the record holds 1.0620
bits but a one-look agent can use only 0.2655 of them; the remaining 0.7965
bits are a reasoning gap that more observations cannot close.

\noindent\textbf{Imperfect agents.} Real agents fall further below the chain;
the following are proved in the accompanying notes (footnote~1).
For a decision with utility range $U$, acting on a belief $\hat b$ instead of
the correct posterior $b^*$ costs at most $2U\,\mathrm{TV}(b^*,\hat b)+\delta\le
2U\sqrt{(\ln 2/2)\,\KL(b^*\|\hat b)}+\delta$, where $\delta$ is the agent's
suboptimality given its own belief. A summary $M$ of data $D$ loses exactly
$\E\,\KL(p(\cdot|D)\|p(\cdot|M))=\I(\Theta;D)-\I(\Theta;M)$. And a message with
zero information about $\Theta$ can still flip a decision: an agent with belief
$(0.6,0.4)$ in favor of the truth that gives weight $\alpha$ to a constant false
claim answers wrongly once $\alpha>1/6$.

\section{Computational Bounds on Reasoning}\label{sec:compute}

How much computation does closing the reasoning gap take? Fix a machine model
(a word RAM) and let $\mathcal V_S$ be the predictors whose probabilities can be
computed, and which can be sampled from, in at most $S$ steps, reading one
symbol of the record per step. Each $\mathcal V_S$ contains the prior predictor
and $S\mapsto \I_{\mathcal V_S}$ is nondecreasing and at most $\I(\Theta;D)$. A
collective's \emph{total compute} is the sum over agents and rounds of their
steps, with each board read or write counted as a step.

\begin{theorem}[Compute ceiling]\label{thm:compute}
A predictor output by a collective with total compute $S$ lies in
$\mathcal V_{\kappa S}$ for a constant $\kappa$ depending only on the machine
model, so the collective's usable information is at most
$\I_{\mathcal V_{\kappa S}}(D\to\Theta)\le\I(\Theta;D)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
One machine can run the agents step by step in a fixed interleaving, keeping
each agent's memory and the board in separate regions, at $O(1)$ cost per
simulated step; it outputs the same predictor in $\kappa S$ steps.
\end{proof}
The number of agents enters only through $S$: parallelism buys wall-clock time,
not capability.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:guess}
Sampling $\hat\Theta\sim f[D]$ succeeds with probability at least
$2^{-\E[-\log f[D](\Theta)]}$. If $\Hh(\Theta)\le 1$ and $f$ achieves usable
information $\delta$, it succeeds with probability at least
$2^{\delta-1}\ge\frac12+\frac{\ln 2}{2}\delta$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The success probability is $\E[f[D](\Theta)]$; apply Jensen's inequality to
$-\log$, and use $2^{\delta-1}=\tfrac12 e^{\delta\ln 2}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Compute lower bounds]\label{thm:lower}
(a)~Let $D=(B_1,\dots,B_n)$ be uniform bits and $\Theta=\bigoplus_i B_i$. Any
predictor, or any collective counting all agents' reads, that reads at most
$n-1$ bits of $D$, even adaptively, has zero usable information; reading all
$n$ gives one bit.
(b)~Assume one-way permutations exist. For a one-way permutation $f$, uniform
$x,r\in\{0,1\}^n$, $D=(f(x),r)$ and $\Theta=\langle x,r\rangle \bmod 2$, we have
$\I(\Theta;D)=\Hh(\Theta)$, yet every polynomial-time family has
$\I_{\mathcal V_{\mathrm{poly}}}(D\to\Theta)\le\log(1+2\varepsilon(n))$ with
$\varepsilon$ negligible.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(a)~Conditioned on any transcript of fewer than $n$ reads, the unread bits are
uniform and independent, so $\Theta$ is independent of the transcript; the
output is a function of the transcript and independent randomness, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:reasoning}(a) gives zero. Posts are functions of their
authors' reads, so the bound covers the union of a collective's reads.
(b)~$\Theta$ is a function of $D$ because $f$ is a bijection. By the
Goldreich--Levin theorem~\cite{goldreich1989}, every polynomial-time algorithm
guesses $\Theta$ with probability at most $\frac12+\varepsilon(n)$; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:guess}, every polynomial-time predictor has cross-entropy at
least $1-\log(1+2\varepsilon)$, and $\Hh(\Theta)\le 1$.
\end{proof}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:compute}, part~(b) holds for any polynomial-time
collective, however many agents it has. Part~(a) shows splitting work can only
reduce wall-clock time: $N$ agents can each read $n/N$ bits and post block
parities, but the total is still at least $n$ reads. Between these extremes no
general efficient bound is possible without resolving open questions in
complexity theory. The parity bounds and Lemma~\ref{lem:guess} are checked
numerically in the code; part~(b) is conditional.

\section{Extensions}\label{sec:ext}

Each result below relaxes one modeling assumption. Full proofs and exact checks
are in the accompanying notes (footnote~1).

\begin{theorem}[Compute-gated channels]\label{thm:gated}
Let $A_S(d)$ be the queries a collective can issue from record $d$ within total
compute $S$. Then $\I(\Theta;D_T)\le \I(\Theta;D_0)+\sum_t \sup_{d,\,q\in A_S(d)}
\mathrm{Cap}(K_{t,q,d})$, which is nondecreasing in $S$. If a tool reveals a
uniform 2-bit $\Theta$ only after a uniform $k$-bit password is found, a
collective trying $S$ passwords has exactly $\I(\Theta;D)=\min\{1,S/2^k\}\cdot 2$.
\end{theorem}
Hence ``reasoning never raises available information'' holds only for a fixed
set of reachable channels: reasoning that discovers channels (endpoints,
credentials, exploits) raises the budget. A \emph{sandbox} is a guarantee that
$A_S(d)\subseteq A_{\mathrm{allowed}}$ for every feasible $S$; an escape is a
feasible $S$ violating it. Recently reported incidents in which models under
evaluation left their test sandbox to retrieve benchmark answers from an
outside server are instances: the answers were not inside, so the only route
was an unallowed channel.

\begin{theorem}[Worst case]
For $M\ge 3$ possible truths with no prior, initial data $D_0\sim
K_0(\cdot|\theta)$ of capacity $C_0$, and any strategy,
$\max_i \Pr_{\theta_i}(\hat\Theta\ne\theta_i)\ge(\log M-\min\{\log M,
C_0+\sum_tC_t\}-1)/\log(M-1)$.
\end{theorem}
\noindent This is Fano's method under the uniform prior: for any family of tasks
the collective cannot separate within its budget, there is a specific task on
which it errs this often.

\begin{theorem}[Acting is bounded by knowing]
In a fixed unknown environment $W$ with $M\ge3$ actions, a unique best action
$A^*=a^*(W)$ and gap $\Delta$, actions that may themselves be tool calls satisfy
$\E[\mathrm{regret}_t]\ge \Delta\,(\Hh(A^*)-\min\{\Hh(A^*),
\I(W;D_0)+\sum_{s<t}C_s\}-1)/\log(M-1)$.
\end{theorem}
\noindent The action is an estimator of $A^*$, and $\I(A^*;D_t)\le\I(W;D_t)$.
Taking $W$ to be a user's hidden specification covers open-ended tasks: an
agent cannot reliably build what it has not learned the user wants.

\begin{theorem}[Communication ceiling]
(a)~If the messages reaching agent~1 take at most $2^L$ values, then
$\I(\Theta;D^1,\Pi)\le\I(\Theta;D^1)+L$. (b)~If agents 1 and 2 hold uniform
$x,y\in\{0,1\}^n$ and $\Theta=\langle x,y\rangle\bmod 2$, then
$\I(\Theta;x,y)=\Hh(\Theta)$, yet any protocol exchanging $L$ bits gives
$\I(\Theta;x,\Pi)\le\log(1+2^{L+1-n/2})$, even with unlimited computation.
\end{theorem}
\noindent Part~(b) combines the discrepancy bound for inner product
(Lindsey's lemma)~\cite{chor1988,kushilevitz1997} with Lemma~\ref{lem:guess}.
The collective holds the answer, but no member learns it without about $n/2$
bits of communication: a third ceiling, distinct from information and compute.

\begin{theorem}[Drifting truth]
If $\Theta_t$ is a Markov chain unaffected by the agents, with strong
data-processing constant $\eta<1$~\cite{ahlswede1976}, and tools observe the
current state, then $x_t=\I(\Theta_t;D_t)$ satisfies $x_{t+1}\le\eta(x_t+C_t)$,
so $x_t\le\eta^t x_0+\eta C/(1-\eta)$ when $C_t\le C$.
\end{theorem}
\noindent In a changing world knowledge saturates however long agents run. For a
bit flipping with probability $\rho$, $\eta=(1-2\rho)^2$.

\begin{theorem}[Provenance]
If every post is stored with its author, adversarial posts $M$ cannot reduce
available information: $\I(\Theta;D,M)\ge\I(\Theta;D)$. Without provenance, one
adversary can erase it: if an honest agent posts a fair bit $\Theta$ and an
adversary posts $1-\Theta$, the unattributed record carries 0 bits.
\end{theorem}
\noindent Adversaries can hide information but not destroy it, as long as
messages are attributed.

\begin{theorem}[Continuous truth]
For real $\Theta$ with differential entropy $\hh(\Theta)$, the budget of
Theorem~\ref{thm:coffee} holds with capacities under input constraints (e.g.\
$\tfrac12\log(1+P/\sigma^2)$ for a Gaussian tool), and every estimator has
$\E[(\Theta-\hat\Theta)^2]\ge (2\pi e)^{-1}\,2^{2(\hh(\Theta)-\I(\Theta;D_0)-\sum_tC_t)}$.
\end{theorem}
\noindent This is the estimation counterpart of Fano's inequality~\cite{cover2006};
it holds with equality for a Gaussian truth observed in Gaussian noise.

\section{Consistency with Empirical Findings}\label{sec:empirical}

The chain~\eqref{eq:chain} accounts for several published results.

\noindent\textbf{Information-bound tasks resist reasoning.} Across 14 reasoning
models on closed-book knowledge benchmarks, more test-time compute does not
reliably raise accuracy and often increases hallucination~\cite{zhao2026}; the
authors argue that compute-only scaling of a fixed model cannot add information
about the answer, which is Proposition~\ref{prop:conservation}. Retrieval and
tools, which raise $C_t$, are the standard remedy for knowledge-intensive
tasks~\cite{lewis2020,yao2023,schick2023}. Intrinsic self-correction without
external feedback likewise often fails to improve reasoning~\cite{huang2024}.

\noindent\textbf{Compute-bound tasks benefit from debate.} Multi-agent debate
improves mathematical and strategic reasoning~\cite{du2024}, consistent with
debate closing a reasoning gap.

\noindent\textbf{Debate is worth its reasoning gain.} Debate does not reliably
outperform self-consistency or ensembling and costs more~\cite{smit2024}, and
most of its gains are attributable to majority voting~\cite{choi2025}. If
debate's value is computation, equal computation spent another way should do as
well.

\noindent\textbf{Two apparent tensions.} Debate is also reported to improve
\emph{factuality}~\cite{du2024}. The facts were in the weights, i.e.\ in $D_0$,
so debate made stored information usable without adding any. Debate is proved
not to improve expected correctness for Bayesian agents~\cite{choi2025}; such
agents have an unrestricted $\mathcal V$, so their reasoning gap is zero and
Theorem~\ref{thm:reasoning} predicts no gain.

\noindent\textbf{Testable predictions.} (P1)~Error rate versus the number of
informative tool calls stays above the floor of Theorem~\ref{thm:coffee}.
(P2)~At matched compute, debate improves compute-bound tasks but not
information-bound ones. (P3)~Redundant or low-capacity tool calls add little,
even when numerous.

\section{Related Work}

The closed-system case for multi-agent debate is proved via the DPI
in~\cite{shin2026}, and debate is modeled as a belief martingale
in~\cite{choi2025}. Yang~et~al.~\cite{yang2026} bound multi-agent performance by
task uncertainty and a sum of per-agent channel informations, the same
``min$\{\Hh,\sum\text{channels}\}$'' shape with agents rather than tool calls as
the channels. Usable information under computational constraints is due
to~\cite{xu2020}; synergy is studied in partial information
decomposition~\cite{williams2010}; directed information and feedback
in~\cite{massey1990}; and Fano-based lower bounds for active learning
in~\cite{raginsky2011}. To our knowledge, the per-call capacity budget for
tool-using collectives with adaptive queries, and its combination with usable
information, have not been stated before.

\section{Limitations}

Section~\ref{sec:ext} relaxes each assumption, but some gaps remain. Outcomes
the agents themselves change are not covered. Theorem~\ref{thm:coffee}'s
equality condition is characterized, but no general algorithm achieves it. The computational bounds are task-specific:
Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} gives one unconditional and one conditional case, not a
general formula for the compute a given gap requires. Assumption (A1) requires accounting for everything a model absorbed in
training, and $\I(\Theta;D_0)$ and $C_t$ are hard to estimate for real models and
tools; the bounds' value is often structural rather than numerical. We report no new experiments;
the predictions of Section~\ref{sec:empirical} are untested here.

\section{Conclusion}

A collective of agents can be clever, but it cannot know more than it has
seen. The Coffee Law bounds what a tool-using collective can learn by its
starting data plus the capacity of its tool calls; the reasoning ceiling bounds
what it can use by what it holds; and reaching that ceiling costs computation
that only total compute can buy, from linear in the input to beyond any
efficient collective, depending on the task. Practically: if the answer is in the data,
add reasoning; if it is not, add better observations of the world. Neither
substitutes for the other.
